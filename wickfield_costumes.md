# Wickfield Eight — Costume Descriptions
### Including Brownout and Kade's Enforcers

Visual reference for the Wickfield Eight one-shot. These are original designs for original
characters — nothing here borrows any real publisher's copyrighted costume, logo, or likeness.
Each entry covers silhouette, materials, palette, and how the character's powers (or lack of
them) show up in the look itself, so an artist or token-maker has enough to work from without
more direction.

---

## Bulwark — Marcus "Mo" Odom

Deliberately unglamorous, built out of what a dockworker actually has access to: a heavy
canvas-and-denim jacket reinforced at the shoulders and forearms with layered padding (less
armor than work gear, the kind of thing you'd wear to move freight, just doubled up), sleeves
cut off or rolled well past the elbow so his forearms are never covered. Faded indigo and rust-
orange — the colors of a shipping container, not a hero's palette — with a wide reflective strap
crossing the chest, salvaged from a safety vest, the one deliberately "costume" touch he allows
himself. Steel-toed boots, fingerless work gloves reinforced across the knuckles. No mask — Mo
doesn't hide his face, on principle. When he braces for an impact, the padding across his
shoulders and forearms is the visual tell that something is about to not move.

## Ricochet — Dani Reyes

Built for motion first: a fitted, stretch-fabric bodysuit in gymnastics-competition style
(she altered one of her old leotards' cut rather than start from nothing), electric yellow-green
fading to violet at the extremities — a gradient that reads as motion blur even standing still.
Wide reflective piping traces her limbs' full range of motion, catching light exactly where a
kinetic bolt would leave her hand or foot. Fingerless gloves and low ankle wraps instead of full
boots, for grip. No cape, nothing loose that could catch on anything mid-tumble. A small
scuffed knee pad on each leg — practical leftovers from her gymnastics days that never got
retired. Hair pulled back hard in a braid or high ponytail, always in motion a half-second after
she's stopped.

## Wisp — Théo Bellamy

Aerodynamic by necessity: a matte, wind-resistant courier jacket (he modified his actual work
jacket rather than buy a costume) in deep slate grey with a single sharp streak of electric cyan
down each sleeve and up the spine, the one flash of color that blurs into a visible contrail when
he's at speed. Fitted cycling leggings, not baggy pants that would flap uselessly at his real
speeds. Wraparound tinted goggles pushed up on his forehead when stationary, snapped down the
instant he moves. His actual bike courier bag, slung diagonally across the back, kept out of
habit more than necessity — he still does the odd delivery between fights. Compression gloves,
low-profile trainers built for grip rather than cushioning. Nothing on him is loose; everything
is cut close enough to disappear at speed.

## Nightglass — Priya Anand

Quiet and precise, like the restoration work she does for a living. A deep charcoal, almost-black
coat with a subtle woven pattern only visible at certain angles — like conservation-grade fabric,
which is more or less what it started as. No bright colors anywhere; the only accent is a thin
line of dull antique-gold thread at the collar and cuffs, echoing gilt frame trim. When Umbral
power moves through her, faint threads of true black — darker than the fabric, darker than
shadow has any right to be — ripple briefly along those gold lines before fading. A soft cowl she
can pull up to obscure the lower half of her face, left down more often than not; she's not
trying to look mysterious, she just prefers not being watched. Thin leather gloves, the kind an
art restorer already owns. Nothing ornamental. Nothing that asks to be noticed.

## Circuit — Owen Park

Visibly homemade, in the good way: a scavenged flight-jacket base, patched and re-stitched at the
seams, with the gauntlet blaster — the actual centerpiece — dominating his right forearm in
exposed matte-black housing, hand-soldered wiring deliberately left visible rather than covered,
a few LEDs blinking status colors only he fully understands. Faded safety-orange and gunmetal
grey, the palette of a workshop rather than a hero. Protective goggles worn up on a headband,
flipped down over one eye when he's aiming through the gauntlet's HUD. The support drone, when
deployed, is a fist-sized quadcopter in the same scavenged-gunmetal finish with one blinking cyan
running light — visibly built by the same hands as the gauntlet. Fingerless gloves on the off
hand only; the gauntlet hand doesn't need one.

## Amberlight — Sable Okafor

Warm where everyone else on the team runs cool: a fitted, dove-white bodysuit with a soft
overlay reminiscent of scrub-fabric structure, seamed in warm amber-gold rather than trauma-red.
The gold isn't paint or fabric so much as light — faint, always-on luminescence traced along the
seams and cuffs like an afterglow, brightening to a genuine warm radiance when she channels Warm
Light or Aegis of Light, dimming back to a soft glow at rest. A short, practical half-cape at the
shoulders (never full-length — she's a working nurse, she knows better than to wear something
that could get grabbed) in the same amber tone, sheer enough to move like heat-shimmer rather
than cloth. No mask; her calm, present face is half of what her power actually does. Soft-soled
shoes built for a twelve-hour hospital shift, because functionally, that's still what she's on.

## Permafrost — Kai Nordahl

Built from his two lives at once: a fitted thermal base layer in deep glacier-blue, echoing old
hockey compression gear, with a barista's canvas apron reworked into a chest panel — he kept the
coffee stains on purpose. Frost visibly crystallizes across his shoulders and forearms when his
power is active, a fine rime that spreads from his hands outward and melts back within a minute
of stillness. No helmet (a pointed choice, after hockey), just short hair silvered faintly at the
temples from repeated frost exposure. Fingerless gloves that end in bare fingertips, deliberately
— he likes the tactile feedback of ice forming directly off his skin. Practical boots with actual
tread, because he's slipped on real ice enough times to never trust a costume choice that looks
good and doesn't grip.

## The Latch — Renata "Ren" Cruz

No powers, no illusions about needing a "costume" beyond good gear: dark tactical-style pants and
a fitted, reinforced jacket in matte black and deep security-guard navy, the kind of outfit that
reads as competent rather than theatrical. A subtle latch-and-hasp emblem — her one concession to
branding — stamped small on the left shoulder, echoing the Community Center's actual door
hardware. Twin collapsible batons holstered cross-draw at the hips, visibly worn from use, not
prop-shiny. Knee and elbow pads under the fabric rather than over it. Close-cropped hair or a
tight bun, nothing that can be grabbed. No mask; she works security at the Center under her own
face already, and doesn't see the point in hiding it now.

## Brownout — Rosalind "Ross" Kade

Once a work uniform, now something else wearing it: a Kade Electric coverall, the embroidered
company name on the chest still legible under grime and small scorch marks, sleeves pushed to the
forearms where faint copper-colored filaments have grown into her skin like veins, brightening to
a dull ember glow when she drains something nearby. Her tool belt is still on, out of habit,
though half the tools have been replaced by things that hum. Where the coverall has torn at the
shoulder, exposed skin shows the same copper tracery climbing toward her collarbone. Her eyes
carry a faint amber afterglow, like a filament that never fully cools. She hasn't washed the
grime off in weeks; on some level she doesn't want to look finished. As a fight escalates and
Power Surge stacks, the copper glow brightens and spreads visibly further up her neck and arms —
a built-in tell for players that she's closing in on Full Strength.

## Kade's Enforcer

A uniform template, deliberately generic — this is a look for the role, not one person. Plain
dark work jackets over civilian clothes, no two identical, unified only by a cheap embroidered
patch (a stylized "K") stitched crookedly on wherever Kade could get it sewn on cheap. Baseball
caps or beanies, sunglasses even at night, the universal shorthand for hired-and-unbothered. A
holstered pistol worn openly rather than concealed — they're not hiding what they do, just doing
it for a paycheck. Nothing about the look suggests loyalty or belief; it's closer to a work
uniform for a job nobody's proud of, which is exactly the point.
